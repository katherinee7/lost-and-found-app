# P5 — Backtrack User Testing

[Deployed Backtrack app](https://backtrack.mit-sdg.dev/)

## Task list

| Task | Instructions to participant | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Report a missing item | You lost a navy JanSport backpack yesterday between 2 and 4 pm. You think you left it in Hayden Library. It has a yellow keychain on the front zipper. Use Backtrack to report it missing. | This tests how well a first-time user knows how to fill out the missing report. Do the fields in the form align with what a person would want to report?|
| 2. Edit the report | A friend reminds you that you still had the backpack when you left Hayden. You now think you left it in the Student Center. Update your report to reflect what you remember. | See where participants expect to find their past reports and whether they can figure out how to edit it. |
| 3. Look for an owner | Today around noon, you found a black Hydro Flask water bottle in the Stata Center. Use Backtrack to see whether anyone might be looking for it. | Observe whether users can start from the finder's side of the app, enter details that narrow the search, and make sense of a search with no results. |
| 4. Keep the item for later | You could not find any matches for the missing bottle. Keep track of the item in Backtrack so you can check again later. | Test whether saving the item is discoverable when the search comes up empty, and whether users can tell that its details were saved. |
| 5. Check again for the owner | Imagine some time has passed. Check whether anyone might now be looking for the bottle you found. | See whether users can get back to the saved item without re-entering its details and understand what a possible match tells them. |
| 6. Arrange a handoff | Contact the person you think might own the bottle. Ask them for a detail that would help you tell whether it's theirs, then agree on a campus pickup spot. | Observe how users go from a potential match to messaging. |
| 7. Confirm the return | Imagine you've just handed the bottle back. Use Backtrack to record your part of the return. Tell me whether the return is complete and how you can tell. | Test whether users can find the confirmation action and understand when the appropriate time is to use it.

### My setup

- Use a separate study account for each participant. Populate the deployed
  app with necessary reports, but check that the bottle search starts
  with no compatible report in each session.
- After Task 4, create the matching missing report from a separate owner
  account.
- In Task 7, wait for the participant to explain the status after their
  confirmation. Then confirm from the owner account and ask what changed.

## Study report 1

Participant 1 completed the missing backpack report without help. They paused at the time fields because they remembered leaving Hayden around 4 p.m. but did not know when they last had the backpack. They entered the full window from the prompt. In the debrief, they said they would have been less comfortable choosing a range in real life because they were unsure whether a wider range would make a match less likely. When asked to correct the location, they went straight to My Missing Reports and checked the updated card after saving. The card gave them enough feedback to move on.

The bottle search displayed a different problem. The participant initially thought the empty result meant that they had entered something wrong. They went back and removed the brand since the owner might have left it blank. After the second search also came up empty, they noticed Save for later and saved the bottle. When asked to check again, they first returned to the search form and began entering the details again. They found the saved item after looking through the rest of the page. In the debrief, they said they had expected it under My Missing Reports because they thought of it as something they had reported.

Once they found the new match, they asked the owner to describe the bottle before proposing a pickup spot. They understood that the match suggested whom to contact and that the owner's answer was what helped establish ownership.

## Study report 2

Participant 2 created the backpack report quickly, but updating it took longer. After opening their report, they read the original location aloud, then went back to the page where they had created it. They thought correcting the location might mean submitting a replacement report. After returning to the existing report and looking at its available actions, they found the edit option and updated it without help. During the debrief, they explained that their first priority had been to avoid leaving two reports for the same backpack. Their hesitation came from uncertainty about where to make the correction.

The participant dealt with the empty bottle search more smoothly. They saved the bottle right away and later went back to the same area to check it. After seeing a candidate, they sent a message asking for a distinguishing mark. When the owner replied with a description of the dent, they proposed meeting at the Stata lobby. They said the exchange felt natural because they would ask the same question in person before handing over the bottle.

The return confirmation was harder to interpret. After recording their side of the handoff, the participant said they were done. When asked what would happen next, they reread the status and realized that the owner still needed to confirm. After the moderator confirmed from the owner account, the participant noticed the point award but was unsure whether the bottle was still in their active saved items. In the debrief, they said the point told them they had received credit, but they still wanted a clear indication that the record was closed.

## Design findings

| Issue or opportunity | Evidence and likely cause | Proposed improvement | Level | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Users might think an empty search is an input mistake. | Participant 1 removed the brand and searched again. An empty result leaves it unclear whether nobody has reported the item or the details were too restrictive. | Explain that no current reports match the entered details and present saving the item as a next step. | Linguistic | Minor |
| Users might look for saved found items near their missing reports. | Participant 1 expected their saved bottle under My Missing Reports and started a new search before finding it. The app groups saved items with the finder workflow, while the participant grouped them with records they had created. | Add a direct link to saved found items from the save confirmation or the main navigation. | Conceptual | Moderate |
| Editing an existing report can be hard to discover. | Participant 2 went back to the creation form before finding the edit option. They remembered where they had entered the details but did not immediately see how to change them from the existing report. | Make Edit report visible next to the item title on the report detail page. | Physical | Minor |
| The handoff status and difference between a person's confirmation and a completed return can be confusing. | Participant 2 initially thought their confirmation completed the process. Once both people confirmed, they noticed the point but could not tell whether the saved record had closed. | Show two explicit states near the confirmation action: “You confirmed. Waiting for the owner.” and “Return complete. This saved item is now closed.” | Conceptual | Moderate |
